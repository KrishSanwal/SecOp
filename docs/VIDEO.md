# 30-second demo video

Rules from the submission guide: 30 s max, no NASA logos, nothing fabricated. Film on the phone in airplane mode so the icon is in the status bar the whole time.

## Setup (10 min, before any filming)
- Two phones: phone A runs the app, phone B films. Or scrcpy on the laptop and screen-record.
- Phone A: open https://getsecop.vercel.app, tap Try demo, run one exam once so the camera and sensor permissions are already granted. Then airplane mode ON. Brightness max.
- Lighting: a window or a desk lamp on the face, no light behind. Dark eyes need the flash, which the app does.
- Record every clip 2 to 3 times. Cut in CapCut or the phone's editor. Captions on, no music louder than the voice.

## Script, filmed apart (Krish edits)

Everyone films **landscape**, phone sideways, 1080p, in a quiet room with a window or lamp in front of the face. Say each line twice in one clip, pause two seconds between takes. Look at the lens. Screen recordings are the phone's built-in recorder, portrait is fine.

## Live pitch, 2 minutes, same structure
1. **What it is (10 s).** "SecOp is a five-minute health check two astronauts give each other with a phone. It works with no internet, and it compares you with your own normal, not a textbook."
2. **The problem, with numbers (20 s).** "A Mars message takes 11 minutes each way. There is no doctor. NASA's behavioral health evidence report says crews under-report symptoms, and the person worst placed to notice slow decline is you. Your crewmate sees it first."
3. **Show it (60 s).** Live, phone mirrored, airplane mode on: pupil reflex with the flash, pulse off the camera with the buzz, balance on the chest. Then Asha's result card: "Right pupil slow, heart rate up 36 on standing, reactions 25% slower than her normal. Here is what the ISS checklist says to do now, and here is the note queued for the surgeon, 11 minutes out."
4. **Why it holds up (20 s).** "Limits come from NASA's ISS Medical Checklist and NASA-STD-3001. Every threshold is in one file a flight surgeon can tune. 65 automated tests. No cloud, no account, no wearable required. Nothing leaves the phone unless the crew sends it."
5. **Close (10 s).** "Next is an analog-mission pilot and a red filter that makes phone pupillometry work on dark eyes. Same app works in Antarctica and in a village clinic. We're Vortexx. SecOp."

## Phone-screen clips that always work
If the live sensors misbehave on camera, these are the safe takes and they are still honest:
- Result card: home → Try demo → tap Asha's latest exam. Scroll slowly.
- Pupil curve: /lab/pupil in a lit room. The curve appears whether or not the reading is clean.
- Pulse wave: /lab/hr. Keep the finger light. The wave draws from the first second.
- Mars line and countdown: bottom of any result card.
- QR loop: home → "Share or receive records with another phone".
